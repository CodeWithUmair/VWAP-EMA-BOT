# The Complete CRT + TBS Trading Strategy Manual

## 1. Executive Overview: The Foundational Logic of CRT and TBS

The integration of Candle Range Theory (CRT) and Turtle Body Soup (TBS) represents a professional-grade quantitative framework designed to exploit institutional manipulation phases. In an environment where retail participants are frequently used as exit liquidity, this strategy functions as a high-confluence filter to identify and trade alongside the "Smart Money." By merging range-based liquidity identification (CRT) with aggressive sweep validation (TBS), traders can achieve a statistically significant win rate of 80–90%, provided the execution adheres to strict institutional footprints.

The core logic of this strategy rests on the **AMD Cycle** (Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution). CRT serves as the diagnostic tool for identifying the initial range where liquidity is engineered before the actual market move occurs.

### The AMD Cycle

| Phase Name | Market Characteristic | Professional Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Accumulation** | Position building within a range (1st Candle) | Wait; identify CRT High/Low boundaries |
| **Manipulation** | Intentional break of range to trap retail (2nd Candle) | Identify the Sweep; wait for TBS confirmation |
| **Distribution** | Explosive reversal toward the terminal target | Execute; manage trade toward opposite boundary |

Central to this identification is the **Second Candle Rule**. In any three-candle sequence, the second candle is designated as the primary manipulator. This candle acts as the "guilty party," breaking the established range to hunt stops and lure breakout traders. By isolating this candle, a strategist prevents premature entries and ensures they are positioned for the distribution phase rather than the manipulation trap.

## 2. Advanced Points of Interest (POI): Identifying High-Value Levels

Success in CRT execution is contingent upon its alignment with institutional Points of Interest (POI). This strategy does not operate on "naked" charts; it requires price to interact with structural footprints where large-scale buy/sell orders are clustered.

### Fair Value Gaps (FVG)

The FVG is a three-candle imbalance representing a price vacuum that the market must eventually fill.

- **Bullish FVG Formula:** The unfilled gap between the High of the 1st Candle and the Low of the 3rd Candle.
- **Bearish FVG Formula:** The unfilled gap between the Low of the 1st Candle and the High of the 3rd Candle.

### Order Blocks (OB)

Order Blocks represent zones of high institutional activity. A professional must distinguish between the "Respect" and "Disrespect" of these levels:

- **Respect:** Price hits the OB and shows immediate wick rejection or a body close that is contained within the zone. This signals a high-probability reversal.
- **Disrespect:** A strong, full-bodied candle breaks through the OB, and subsequent candles sustain a close beyond it. This invalidates the level's original intent.

### The Inverse FVG (S/R Flip)

The Inverse FVG occurs when a previously established FVG is "disrespected." When price closes decisively through a bearish FVG, that zone flips from resistance to a potent support zone. These inverted levels are exceptionally strong because they represent a failed institutional defense; the market has effectively "flipped" its bias, turning old imbalances into high-conviction entry zones.

## 3. The Superiority of the "Body Soup": TBS vs. TWS

The primary differentiator of this high-win-rate strategy is the technical distinction between a "Body" sweep and a "Wick" sweep. This determines whether price has truly harvested liquidity or merely tested it.

- **Turtle Wick Soup (TWS):** A candle wicks beyond a key level but closes back within the range. This is a simple liquidity sweep and carries lower probability.
- **Turtle Body Soup (TBS):** A candle closes its full body beyond the liquidity level (the previous swing or CRT boundary).

**The "Body Soup" Advantage:** The TBS candle is the "guilty party." By closing the body beyond a key level, the market traps the maximum possible volume of retail participants. Breakout traders see the body close as a signal of strength and enter "long" at the exact moment institutional players are preparing to "short" the distribution. This aggressive stop-hunt creates the fuel for an explosive reversal. Identifying the TBS close is the final trigger that a deep-seated liquidity grab has been completed.

## 4. Multi-Timeframe Alignment: From Context to Execution

Timeframe alignment is non-negotiable for risk mitigation. The Higher Timeframe (HTF) establishes the narrative and bias, while the Lower Timeframe (LTF) provides the surgical entry.

### Top-Tier Combinations

- **4H (Context) + 15m (Execution):** The primary professional standard for intraday setups.
- **Daily (Context) + 1H (Execution):** Optimized for high-conviction swing-biased moves.
- **1H (Context) + 5m/15m (Execution):** For rapid intraday execution.

**The Contextual Flow:** A strategist first identifies an FVG or OB on the HTF. Execution is only permitted when price "taps" or enters this HTF zone. Once the tap occurs, the trader shifts to the LTF to wait for the mechanical CRT candle formation and the subsequent TBS manipulation.

## 5. Mechanical Execution: The Step-by-Step Entry and Exit Protocol

Trading consistency is achieved through a rigid, "pre-flight" checklist. Each trade must meet these criteria:

1. **Level Identification:** Price must tap an FVG or OB on the HTF.
2. **CRT Marking:** Identify the CRT Candle (the first candle to tap the POI). Mark the CRT High and CRT Low.
3. **Equilibrium Calculation:** Apply a Fibonacci 0.5 level to the CRT candle to find the Equilibrium Line.
4. **The Sweep (TBS Candle):** Wait for an LTF candle to sweep the CRT boundary (High for sells, Low for buys) and close with a full body beyond it.
5. **Trigger:** Mark the High (for buys) or Low (for sells) of that TBS Candle. Enter only when a subsequent candle breaks that TBS mark.
   - **Rule:** This break must occur ideally within 1–2 candles. If price stagnates for 5+ candles, the setup decays in probability and is invalidated.
6. **Risk Management:** Set the Stop Loss (SL) immediately beyond the extreme sweep point of the TBS Candle.

### Take Profit (TP) Strategy

- **TP1 (Partial):** Close 50% of the position at the 0.5 Equilibrium Line. This covers risk and secures the trade.
- **TP2 (Terminal):** Close the remaining 50% at the opposite CRT boundary (the High for buys or the Low for sells).

## 6. Pattern Validation: High-Probability M & W Formations

Retail M and W patterns are frequently used as "traps." A CRT trader is strictly prohibited from executing standard patterns; only "Liquidity-Grab" variations are valid.

- **The Valid W-Pattern:** The second leg of the "W" must sweep below the low of the first leg. This ensures that retail "support" buyers are stopped out before the institutional move begins.
- **The Valid M-Pattern:** The second leg of the "M" must sweep above the high of the first leg. This traps "breakout" buyers and hunts the stops of early sellers.

Any M or W formation that fails to produce a clear liquidity sweep is a "Trap" setup and must be avoided. Mastery of the CRT + TBS strategy requires the quantitative discipline to wait for these specific, manipulated patterns to manifest at institutional POIs. Ensure the market reveals its hand through a full-body TBS close before committing any capital.
